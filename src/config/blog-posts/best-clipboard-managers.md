## First: the clipboard history you already have

Before you install anything, check what your system already does. For many people it's enough.

### How to see clipboard history on Windows 11 and 10

Press **Win+V**. The first time, Windows asks you to turn clipboard history on (you can also do it in **Settings → System → Clipboard**). From then on, Win+V shows your recent copies: click one to paste it.

It keeps the last 25 items: text, HTML and images under 4 MB. The list is wiped when you restart, except for items you pin. If you sign in with a Microsoft account, it can sync across your Windows PCs.

### How to see clipboard history on a Mac

Until macOS 26 Tahoe, Macs had no clipboard history at all. On Tahoe, open Spotlight (**⌘+Space**) and press **⌘+4** to see the clipboard view. It's a good start, but it clears old clips after a few hours by default and has no pinning, which is why most Mac users still install one of the apps below.

### Linux

**KDE Plasma** ships with Klipper, a clipboard manager in the system tray (**Meta+V** on Plasma 6). **GNOME** has none built in; the popular fixes are the **Clipboard Indicator** extension and **GPaste**.

## Windows

### Ditto

Ditto is the classic Windows clipboard manager: free, open source, and around for over a decade. Press **Ctrl+`** (the key above Tab) and a searchable list of everything you copied appears.

**What it's best at:** being a better Win+V. It keeps thousands of clips across restarts, searches them as you type, handles text, images and files, and can paste a clip as plain text. It can even share clips between PCs on your network.

**Where others beat it:** it looks dated, and it's Windows-only. If you also use a Mac, CopyQ gives you the same tool on both.

### CopyQ

CopyQ is the power user's clipboard manager. It runs on Windows, Mac and Linux, and it's free and open source.

**What it's best at:** control. Sort clips into tabs, edit them before pasting, and write commands that run on what you copy (strip formatting, open links, save images to a folder). It's the same app on all three systems.

**Where others beat it:** it takes setup. The defaults are plain, and the best parts are hidden behind settings and scripting. If you just want a list of recent copies, Ditto or Maccy are quicker to love.

## Mac

### Maccy

Maccy is the clipboard manager most Mac users are told to install first. It's free and open source (download it from GitHub or Homebrew), or buy it on the App Store to support the developer.

**What it's best at:** staying out of the way. Press **Shift+⌘+C**, type a few letters, press Enter. It's tiny, fast, and keyboard-first, with pinning and an option to ignore passwords.

**Where others beat it:** it's deliberately simple: a list in a menu, with no syncing between devices and only small previews. Paste is far more visual.

### Paste

Paste is the polished, visual option: a strip of cards along the bottom of your screen, with pinboards for things you reuse.

**What it's best at:** syncing. Copy on your Mac, paste on your iPhone or iPad. Previews are rich, and organizing clips into pinboards is easy.

**Where others beat it:** it's a subscription, and it's Apple-only. If you don't need sync, Maccy does the core job for free.

### Pastebot

Pastebot, from the makers of Tweetbot, is a paid Mac app built around cleaning up what you paste.

**What it's best at:** filters. Strip formatting, change case, find and replace or tidy up text as you paste, and paste a list of clips one after another.

**Where others beat it:** it costs money and it's Mac-only. If you don't need filters, Maccy is free.

### Flycut

Flycut is a free, open-source clipboard manager for developers, based on the old Jumpcut.

**What it's best at:** code. Press **Shift+⌘+V** to cycle through recent text clips right where you're typing. Nothing to learn.

**Where others beat it:** it only keeps text, so no images or files, and its features are basic next to Maccy or Paste.

### Raycast and Alfred

If you already use a launcher, you may already have a clipboard manager. Raycast's clipboard history is part of its free plan, on Mac and in the Windows beta. Alfred includes one in its paid Powerpack. Both are good, and both are worth checking before you install another app. We compare them in [Best Raycast Alternatives](/blog/raycast-alternatives-windows).

## Clipboard managers and passwords

A clipboard manager saves what you copy, and sometimes you copy passwords. Before you pick one, check three things:

- **Does it skip password managers?** Maccy, Paste, CoolDesk and most good Mac apps ignore copies a password manager marks as hidden. On Windows, check the settings.
- **Can you pause it?** Most can. Do it before copying anything private.
- **Where does it store your history?** Locally, or in someone's cloud? Sync is useful, but it means your clips leave your computer.

## FAQ

**How do I see my clipboard history?**
On Windows, press Win+V (turn it on the first time). On a Mac with macOS Tahoe, open Spotlight with ⌘+Space and press ⌘+4. On KDE Plasma, click Klipper in the system tray. Older Macs and GNOME need a clipboard manager.

**Where is the clipboard on my computer?**
It isn't a file or a folder. The clipboard lives in memory and holds only your last copy. To see past copies, use your system's clipboard history or a clipboard manager.

**What's the best free clipboard manager?**
Ditto on Windows, Maccy on Mac, and CopyQ if you want one app on Windows, Mac and Linux. All three are free and open source.

**Why is my Windows clipboard history empty after a restart?**
Windows clears clipboard history on restart, except for pinned items. Pin what you want to keep, or use Ditto, which keeps its history across restarts.

**Is it safe to use a clipboard manager?**
Yes, if it skips passwords and stores clips locally. Pick one that ignores password managers, pause it before copying something private, and check whether it syncs your clips to a server.

**Can I sync my clipboard between devices?**
Windows can sync between Windows PCs with a Microsoft account. Apple's Universal Clipboard passes your latest copy between Apple devices; Paste syncs your whole history. Ditto can share clips between PCs on your network.
