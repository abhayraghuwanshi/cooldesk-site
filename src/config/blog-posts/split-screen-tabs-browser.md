# How to Split Screen Tabs in Any Browser (2026)

How to put two browser tabs side by side in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari, Opera, Vivaldi and Zen, which browsers can show more than two, and how to keep docs, a terminal and files next to your pages.

Putting two tabs side by side used to need an extension or two browser windows dragged into place. Not any more. In 2026 almost every major browser can split one window into two pages, and some can do four or more. The hard part now is knowing where each browser hid the option, and what each one can and can't do.

## Chrome

After years of extensions filling the gap, Google added a native **Split View** to desktop Chrome in version 145 (early 2026).

**How to do it:** right-click a tab and pick the split view option from the menu, or right-click a link and open it in split view. Drag the divider in the middle to resize. Controls near the address bar let you swap the two sides or separate them again. If you don't see the option, update Chrome: **⋮ → Settings → About Chrome**.

**What it's best at:** being there. Most people already use Chrome, and now they don't need an extension or a second window to compare two pages.

**Where others beat it:** it's two pages only, and only web pages. Opera, Vivaldi and Zen go to four or more. Some tab managers and session savers don't remember which tabs were paired.

## Microsoft Edge

Edge was the first big browser to build split screen in, and it has had the longest to get it right.

**How to do it:** click the **Split screen** button in the toolbar and choose the second tab, or right-click a link and choose **Open link in split screen window**. If the button is missing, turn it on under **Settings → Appearance → Customize toolbar**. Drag the bar in the middle to resize. To undo it, use the **…** menu on the split and separate the two tabs. Closing one side closes that page.

**What it's best at:** being polished. Links you click on the left can open on the right, which is great for working through search results or a list of tickets.

**Where others beat it:** still two pages at most. If you need a 2×2 grid, look at Opera, Vivaldi or Zen.

## Firefox

Mozilla shipped **Split View** to everyone in Firefox 149 (March 2026), after testing it behind a hidden setting from Firefox 146.

**How to do it:** Ctrl-click (⌘-click on Mac) two tabs to select them, then right-click and choose **Open in Split View**. Or right-click one tab and choose **Add Split View**. To end it, right-click and choose **Separate Split View**, or just close one side.

**What it's best at:** a clean, private-by-default split for people who already like Firefox.

**Where others beat it:** two tabs only, and pinned tabs can't join a split yet. Zen, which is built on Firefox, goes further.

## Safari

Safari has no built-in way to show two tabs inside one window, even on macOS 26 Tahoe.

**How to do it:** use macOS itself. Drag a tab out so it becomes its own window. Then hover over the green button at the top-left of a window and choose to tile it left or right. Do the same with the other window on the opposite side.

**What it's best at:** nothing to install. It works with every Mac app, not just Safari.

**Where others beat it:** these are two windows, not one, so they don't move or close together, and switching to another desktop breaks the layout. Every other browser here does it better inside one window.

## Opera

Opera's **Split Screen** works with two, three or four tabs at once.

**How to do it:** select the tabs you want (Ctrl or Shift + click, ⌘ on Mac), right-click and choose split screen. You can also drag a tab down into the page and drop it where you want it to go.

**What it's best at:** four pages with no setup. Watching a stream next to a chat, or keeping a few dashboards up at once, takes seconds.

**Where others beat it:** you have less control over the layout than in Vivaldi. And like every option above, it can only show web pages.

## Vivaldi

Vivaldi has had **Tab Tiling** for years, and it is still the most flexible split in any mainstream browser.

**How to do it:** select tabs (Ctrl/Shift + click), right-click and choose **Tile tabs**. Pick a vertical, horizontal or grid layout from the status bar. Tab stacks can be tiled with a single click.

**What it's best at:** control. There is no fixed limit on panes, the layouts are flexible, and it fits well with Vivaldi's tab stacks and workspaces.

**Where others beat it:** Vivaldi has a lot of settings. If you just want two pages side by side, Chrome or Edge is quicker to learn.

## Zen

Zen is a Firefox-based browser with a vertical tab bar, built around split view from day one.

**How to do it:** select tabs, right-click and choose split view, or drag one tab onto another. It supports up to four tabs side by side, in a grid or in columns.

**What it's best at:** a calm, keyboard-friendly browser where splitting is a core feature, not something added later. It's a popular new home for people leaving Arc.

**Where others beat it:** it's young, and some Chrome-only sites and extensions don't work. If your work depends on a Chrome extension, Chrome or Edge is safer.

## Works in any browser: let the OS do it

If your browser has no split view, your operating system can still tile two windows:

- **Windows 11:** press **Win + ←** on one window and **Win + →** on the other, or hover over the maximise button and pick a Snap layout.
- **Mac:** hover over the green button and tile left or right.
- **Linux (GNOME/KDE):** drag a window to the left or right edge, or press **Super + ←/→**.

It's two windows instead of one, but it works with every browser and every app.

## FAQ

**How do I split screen tabs in Chrome?**
Update to Chrome 145 or newer, right-click a tab and choose the split view option. Chrome shows two tabs side by side in one window.

**How do I have two tabs side by side in one window?**
Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Opera, Vivaldi and Zen all do this built in. Safari doesn't; there you tile two windows with macOS Split View.

**How do I split screen on Microsoft Edge?**
Click the Split screen button in the toolbar (turn it on under Settings → Appearance if it's hidden), then pick the second tab.

**Which browser can show more than two tabs at once?**
Opera and Zen show up to four. Vivaldi's Tab Tiling has no fixed limit. CoolDesk's multi view shows all your open tabs in a grid.

**Can I split a web page with a terminal or a file?**
Not in a normal browser; they only split web pages. CoolDesk's multi view can put pages, terminals, folders, files and notes side by side.

**Do I need an extension to split screen in Chrome?**
Not any more. Chrome 145 added Split View. Extensions are only needed if you want more than two panes.
